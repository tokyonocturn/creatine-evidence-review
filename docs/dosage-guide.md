# Dosage guide

The short version: **3–5 g of creatine monohydrate per day, every day.** Loading is
optional. Timing barely matters. Consistency matters a lot.

All recommendations below assume creatine **monohydrate** and healthy adults. See
[the safety assessment](safety-assessment.md) for who should not self-supplement.

## 1. The three valid protocols

| Protocol | Dose | Duration to saturation | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Standard maintenance** | 3–5 g/day (about 0.03 g/kg) | About 28 days | Most people; simplest; least GI upset |
| **Loading + maintenance** | 20–25 g/day (0.3 g/kg), split into 4–5 doses, for 5–7 days, then 3–5 g/day | About 5–7 days | Athletes before competition, anyone who wants saturation now |
| **Low-dose** | 3 g/day | About 28 days | Smaller adults; people who prefer a single small scoop |

These are not three different treatments. They reach the same muscle creatine
plateau; they differ in how fast they get there [R01].

## 2. Dose by body weight

Relative dosing is more accurate than a fixed scoop for people at the extremes of
body mass.

| Body weight | Maintenance (0.03 g/kg) | Practical daily dose | Loading (0.3 g/kg) | Loading split |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 50 kg | 1.5 g | 3 g | 15 g | 4 × 3.75 g |
| 60 kg | 1.8 g | 3 g | 18 g | 4 × 4.5 g |
| 70 kg | 2.1 g | 3–5 g | 21 g | 4 × 5.25 g |
| 80 kg | 2.4 g | 3–5 g | 24 g | 4 × 6 g |
| 90 kg | 2.7 g | 5 g | 27 g | 4 × 6.75 g |
| 100 kg | 3.0 g | 5 g | 30 g | 4 × 7.5 g |
| 110+ kg | 3.3+ g | 5–7 g | 33+ g | 4–5 doses |

The 3 g floor reflects the fact that typical creatine turnover is about 1–2 g/day
and that lower doses are poorly studied below 3 g. Very large athletes may need
5–10 g/day to maintain saturated stores [R01]. There is no evidence that dividing
the maintenance dose is necessary; a single daily dose is fine.

## 3. Loading: when it is worth it

**Load if** you have a competition, testing session, or training block starting
within a week and want full saturation immediately.

**Skip the load if** you are starting a long-term habit. The 3–5 g/day protocol
reaches the same place in about four weeks with fewer GI complaints and no need to
remember four doses a day.

Loading does **not** produce a larger long-term effect. It compresses the timeline
[R01; R02].

### Loading without stomach upset

- Split into 4–5 doses of 5 g rather than drinking 20 g at once.
- Take each dose with a meal or a carbohydrate-containing drink.
- Use cold or room-temperature water; creatine is only modestly soluble (about
  14 g/L at 20 °C) [R02].
- If nausea persists, abandon the load and use 5 g/day.

## 4. Timing

**Any consistent time works.** Pre-workout, post-workout, with breakfast, or before
bed all produce similar adaptations in controlled comparisons [R55; R56; R57].

- Post-exercise dosing has a modest mechanistic rationale (greater blood flow and
  insulin-mediated uptake), but trials have not shown a reliable advantage.
- Taking it with a meal containing carbohydrate and protein may improve retention
  slightly, but this is not required for efficacy.
- On rest days, take the same dose. Muscle creatine does not need a "training day"
  schedule.

**The practical rule:** attach creatine to an existing daily habit (morning coffee,
breakfast, post-workout shake) and stop optimising the clock.

## 5. Consistency and missed doses

Muscle creatine stores fall slowly when supplementation stops; full depletion
takes weeks, not days. A missed day is not a crisis.

- Take the missed dose when you remember, unless it is close to the next one.
- Do **not** double up to "catch up"; excess creatine is excreted as creatinine.
- After 4–6 weeks of daily use, stores are saturated. Occasional missed days will
  not undo the adaptation.

## 6. Mixing and storage

| Issue | Practical guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Solubility | About 14 g/L at 20 °C; warm water dissolves more. Some grit is normal. |
| Degradation | Dry powder is stable for years. In solution, creatine converts to creatinine faster with heat and acidity: about 4% at pH 5.5, 12% at pH 4.5, and 21% at pH 3.5 after 3 days at 25 °C [R02; R53]. |
| Best practice | Mix and drink within the same session. Do not prepare a week of creatine drinks. |
| Juice | Acidic juice speeds degradation; if you use it, drink immediately. |
| Coffee/tea | Hot coffee dissolves creatine well; the caffeine interaction is not a reason to avoid it at normal intakes [R58]. |
| Storage | Keep the tub sealed, dry, and out of direct heat. A desiccant packet helps in humid climates. |

## 7. Format and dose practicalities

| Format | Dose reality | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Powder | 1 scoop = 5 g; cheapest by far | Best default |
| Capsules | 0.75–2.5 g per serving; often 2–7 capsules for 5 g | Convenient, 2–6× the cost |
| Gummies | 1–1.5 g per gummy; 3–5 gummies for 5 g; several products underdose | Convenient but expensive and less reliable |
| Liquid | Poor stability; rarely delivers a verified 5 g dose | Avoid |

Creatine HCl capsules typically contain 750 mg of the salt, which supplies about
587 mg of creatine. Matching a 5 g monohydrate dose requires about 7.5 capsules —
which is why HCl is a poor value route even before efficacy is considered
(see [form chemistry](generated/brand-comparison.md#form-chemistry)).

## 8. What to expect, and when

| Timeframe | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| 3–7 days (loading) or 3–4 weeks (no load) | Muscle stores saturate; slight weight gain from intracellular water |
| 1–2 weeks | Strength and power may improve, especially repeated efforts |
| 4–12 weeks | Lean mass advantage emerges when combined with resistance training |
| 12+ weeks | Strength and lean-mass benefits accumulate; cognitive effects, if any, are slower and less certain |

Creatine is not a stimulant. It does not produce an acute "hit" and it will not
compensate for inadequate training, sleep, or protein intake.

## 9. Special populations

| Group | Dose | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Older adults | 5 g/day; 0.3 g/kg loading if supervised | Combine with resistance training; the lean-mass effect depends on it [R14; R15] |
| Women | 3–5 g/day | Same dose as men; lower body mass often means 3 g is enough; no evidence of greater risk [R19; R21] |
| Postmenopausal women | 5 g/day with resistance training | Small lean-mass and leg-press benefits; bone density does not change [R17; R18] |
| Vegetarians and vegans | 3–5 g/day | Lower baseline stores and larger increases; likely the best responders [R31] |
| Larger athletes | 5–10 g/day | Maintenance requirement scales with muscle mass [R01] |
| Adolescents | Clinician/guardian decision | Short-term use appears safe in supervised settings; long-term data are lacking [R02] |
| Pregnancy/breastfeeding | Not established | Do not self-supplement; clinician guidance required [R21; R40] |
| Kidney disease | Avoid unless supervised | Diagnostic confusion and theoretical risk [R36; R40] |

## 10. Common dosing mistakes

1. **Loading with 20 g at once.** Causes bloating and diarrhea; split the dose.
2. **Taking creatine only on training days.** Stores decline; take it daily.
3. **Expecting fat loss.** The scale may go up 1–2 kg from water; that is not fat.
4. **Buying HCl to "avoid bloating".** HCl is not better tolerated in controlled
   trials and costs far more per effective dose [R03; R07].
5. **Mixing a week of drinks in advance.** Creatine degrades to creatinine in
   solution.
6. **Stopping after two weeks because "nothing happened".** Without loading,
   saturation takes about four weeks.
7. **Paying for a blend to get 5 g of creatine.** Buy monohydrate and, if you want
   HMB or tart cherry, buy them separately with evidence in mind.
